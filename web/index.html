    05 / ABOUT THE BENCHMARK

    Simple rules.
    No model judge.

    The game is small. The assumptions shouldn’t be hidden.

    Inspect data & provenance ↗
    01

    Take the last kana.

    Submit one hiragana noun beginning with the previous word’s final normalized kana. No repeated readings. End in ん and you lose.

    02

    Closed book, fixed dictionary.

    Models receive rules and game history, not a word list or tools. A pinned JMdict-derived noun vocabulary checks each move deterministically.

    03

    Separate legality from strategy.

    A legal move can still be weak. TUBELEX frequency mass and alternating discounted lookahead explore move quality, but neither is a validated win probability or human-recall model.

    04

    MemoryBot is our baseline opponent.

    A seeded, frequency-weighted bot with imperfect recall—not another language model. It knows a fixed word pool, avoids words ending in ん, and sometimes forgets that a word has already been played. The rules below describe the current baseline; the historical self-play pilot above is a separate experiment.

    05

    Show what the pilot cannot say.

    One opening and four completed games cannot support broad model rankings. Frequency coverage is incomplete; zero covered options is not necessarily a dictionary dead end.

    CURRENT BASELINE / MEMORYBOT

    Familiar words. Imperfect recall.

    SEEDED · NO SEARCH
    01 / VOCABULARY

    A fixed pool of 7,888 readings

    The current noun-v3 policy selects JMdict-eligible noun readings with unambiguous TUBELEX frequency of at least 1 per million. Earlier noun-v2 runs used 7,878 readings. Words ending in ん are excluded. The LLM is still checked against the larger referee dictionary; it is not restricted to the bot’s pool.

    02 / CHOICE

    Common words are more likely

    For the required starting kana, draw a candidate with probability proportional to its corpus frequency. There is no strategic lookahead. Previously played words remain in the retrieval pool, so they can be drawn again.

    03 / MEMORY

    The last 12 moves are remembered

    Words played by either player in the last 12 moves are recognized perfectly. Older words are recognized with a probability that decays with age: half-life 40 moves, with a 20% floor.

    04 / RETRIEVAL & LOSS

    Recognize, retry—or repeat

    A recognized used word is rejected and another candidate is drawn, without replacement within that turn. There is no fixed attempt cap. A forgotten repeat is submitted and loses. If the entire starting-kana pool is rejected or empty, the bot passes and loses if the referee still has legal words.

    Exact memory rule & reproducibility +

    Let age be the number of globally played moves since a word’s last occurrence, counting both players. Recognition probability is 1 when age ≤ 12. Otherwise, p = 0.2 + 0.8 × 2^(-(age − 12) / 40). A newly played word has age 1 on the next turn. Rejected retrievals do not refresh its memory age.

    The full game history remains visible; noisy recognition is an intentional bot limitation, not a hidden-history condition. Vocabulary, recognition parameters, and RNG seed are fixed per run and recorded in its configuration. Each game starts with fresh bot state; the current smoke pair uses seed 17 in both seat orders.

    This is a reproducible forgetting baseline, not a validated simulation of human play. It never makes spelling or ん-ending mistakes by construction. Report win rate, valid-move rate, game length, and failure type separately: short games alone do not establish strategic strength, and baseline-pool exhaustion is not full-dictionary exhaustion.

    Dataset scope: the Results and Explorer views currently show the earlier LLM self-play snapshot. MemoryBot match results are not yet included in those totals.

    Scoring assumptions & data sources +

    The atlas samples safe words with weights proportional to corpus frequency. With mass reward r and transition matrix P, alternating lookahead solves Q = −r − γPQ. Higher Q is preferable for the player who just moved. The atlas uses log1p mass, threshold 1 per million, and frequency exponent 1.

    The initial graph is frozen for future lookahead; future word depletion is not modeled. Dictionary counts include safe readings, not unique meanings. Ambiguous frequency contributions are excluded from the default pool. Unsupported kana are explicitly marked, never treated as proven traps.
